EDITORIAL ESSAY

Why We’re Reading It

In an age dominated by algorithmic optimization, social metrics, and the relentless pressure toward productive self-improvement, Dostoevsky’s underground man reminds us of something deeply uncomfortable: human consciousness is stubbornly irrational, fiercely protective of free choice, and impossible to reduce to mathematical equations.

Written in 1864 after the death of his first wife and brother, Notes from Underground is Dostoevsky’s explosive polemic against the utopian socialist dreams of nineteenth-century Europe—represented by the legendary "Crystal Palace". The underground man does not oppose utopia because he loves suffering; he opposes it because a perfected, calculated society leaves no room for human volition. If 2 + 2 always equals 4, what is left for the human soul to decide?
